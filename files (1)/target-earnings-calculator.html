What is the Target Earnings Calculator?

The Target Earnings Calculator flips the usual earnings formula around. Instead of starting from your traffic and estimating revenue, you start from a revenue goal and work backward to see exactly how many clicks — and how many pageviews — it will take to get there, given your current CTR and CPC.

This makes it a practical planning tool for setting realistic traffic targets tied directly to a revenue number that matters to your business.

Why This Calculator Matters

Revenue goals are easy to state and hard to translate into action. Knowing you want to earn $2,000 next month doesn't tell you what to do differently — but knowing you need 40,000 more pageviews at your current CTR and CPC gives you a concrete traffic target you can plan content, promotion, or SEO work around.

The Formula

Required Clicks = Target Earnings ÷ CPC
Required Pageviews = Required Clicks ÷ (CTR ÷ 100)

The calculation works in two steps: first it finds how many clicks are needed to reach the revenue goal at your CPC, then it works out how many pageviews are needed to generate that many clicks at your CTR.

Worked Example

To reach $2,000 at a $0.35 CPC, you need 2,000 ÷ 0.35 ≈ 5,714 clicks. At a 2.5% CTR, that requires 5,714 ÷ 0.025 ≈ 228,560 pageviews.

How to Use This Calculator

  • Target Earnings: Set the revenue goal you're planning around, whether monthly or for another period.
  • CTR: Use your actual historical CTR for the most realistic result.
  • CPC: Use your actual average CPC — this has the largest effect on the required click count.

Because CTR and CPC assumptions drive the whole result, try a couple of scenarios — your typical performance and a more conservative one — to understand a realistic range rather than a single number.

Tips for Reaching Your Target Faster

Improving CTR reduces the pageviews needed without requiring any additional traffic growth, so start there if it's underperforming.

Higher-CPC content categories reach the same revenue goal with fewer total clicks — worth factoring into content planning.

Recalculate monthly as your CTR and CPC shift, since a stale assumption can make your traffic target inaccurate.

Common Mistakes

Using an overly optimistic CTR or CPC — often pulled from a single best day — will understate how much traffic is actually needed, setting up an unrealistic plan. It's also worth remembering this is a linear projection: real-world CTR and CPC often shift as traffic scales, so treat the output as a planning estimate rather than a guarantee.
