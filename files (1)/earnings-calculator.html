What is the AdSense Earnings Calculator?

The AdSense Earnings Calculator turns three inputs — pageviews, click-through rate, and cost per click — into a clear revenue projection. Instead of manually multiplying numbers in a spreadsheet, you get an instant breakdown of estimated clicks alongside daily, monthly, and yearly earnings. It's built for bloggers, publishers, and site owners who want a fast, reliable way to understand what their traffic is actually worth.

Because everything runs in your browser, there's nothing to install and nothing to submit. Change any input and recalculate as many times as you like while you model different traffic or CTR scenarios.

Why Earnings Estimation Matters

Publishers rarely have a single, static earnings number — traffic grows, CTR shifts with content type, and CPC moves with advertiser demand and season. Estimating earnings before those numbers fully materialize helps with real decisions: is a new content strategy worth the effort, does a traffic dip justify concern, and how much should a site's growth be worth to a potential buyer or advertiser. A quick, repeatable calculation turns vague intuition into a number you can plan around.

The Formula

Earnings estimation builds from clicks upward. First, clicks are derived from pageviews and CTR, then multiplied by CPC to reach daily revenue:

Clicks = Pageviews × (CTR ÷ 100)
Daily Earnings = Clicks × CPC

Monthly and yearly figures are simple projections from the daily number — daily earnings multiplied by 30 for a month, or by 365 for a year. This keeps the math transparent: every output can be traced back to the three numbers you entered.

Worked Example

A blog with 120,000 monthly pageviews, a 2.5% CTR, and a $0.35 CPC generates 120,000 × 0.025 = 3,000 clicks. At $0.35 per click, that's 3,000 × $0.35 = $1,050 per month, or roughly $35 per day and $12,775 per year at a steady rate.

How to Use This Calculator

  • Pageviews: Pull your total monthly pageviews from Google Analytics or AdSense reporting.
  • CTR: Use your site's actual click-through rate if you have it; otherwise, a conservative estimate between 1% and 3% is typical for most niches.
  • CPC: Enter your average cost per click, which varies widely by niche — finance and insurance sites often see higher CPCs than entertainment or general-interest blogs.

Once entered, hit Calculate to see clicks and projected earnings update instantly. Adjust any field and recalculate to test different scenarios, like what a 0.5% CTR improvement would mean for monthly revenue.

Tips for More Accurate Estimates

Use trailing 30-day averages for CTR and CPC rather than a single best day, which smooths out normal daily volatility.

Recalculate seasonally — CPC often rises in Q4 due to holiday advertiser demand and falls in slower months.

Model a range, not a point estimate: run the calculator with your best-case and worst-case CTR and CPC to understand the spread.

Common Mistakes

The most frequent error is using an unrealistically high CTR or CPC pulled from a single exceptional day rather than a representative average, which inflates projections. A second common mistake is forgetting that earnings scale with pageviews — a traffic drop of 20% translates directly into a 20% earnings drop at constant CTR and CPC, which is easy to overlook when focusing only on ad metrics.
