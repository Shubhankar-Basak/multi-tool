What is the CTR Calculator?

The CTR Calculator measures how often people who see your ads actually click them. Click-through rate is one of the most closely watched AdSense metrics because it directly influences both revenue and, over time, how AdSense's algorithm prioritizes ad placements on your pages.

Enter your total ad impressions and total clicks, and the calculator instantly returns your CTR as a percentage — no manual division required.

Why CTR Matters

CTR is a signal of how relevant and well-placed your ads are relative to your content and audience. A higher CTR generally means more clicks per visitor, which — combined with CPC — drives more revenue from the same amount of traffic. Publishers track CTR over time to catch layout problems, ad blindness, or seasonal shifts in visitor behavior before they show up as a revenue drop.

The Formula

CTR (%) = (Clicks ÷ Impressions) × 100

Impressions count every time an ad is served and viewable on a page, while clicks count the actual interactions. Dividing one by the other and multiplying by 100 converts the ratio into a percentage that's easy to compare across pages, months, or ad units.

Worked Example

A site with 500,000 impressions and 14,000 clicks has a CTR of (14,000 ÷ 500,000) × 100 = 2.80%, which sits comfortably within the typical 1–3% range for content-driven sites.

How to Use This Calculator

  • Impressions: Pull your total ad impressions from the AdSense reporting dashboard for the period you want to analyze.
  • Clicks: Use the matching total clicks figure for the same date range and ad units.

Keep both numbers from the same reporting window — mixing a month of impressions with a week of clicks will produce a misleading CTR.

Tips for Improving CTR

Test ad placement near natural reading breaks, such as after an introduction or between sections, where attention is already focused.

Avoid stacking too many ad units close together, which tends to depress CTR per unit even as total clicks stay flat.

Compare CTR by device — mobile and desktop layouts often perform differently and may need separate placement strategies.

Common Mistakes

A common error is treating CTR as a standalone success metric. A very high CTR paired with a low CPC can still underperform a moderate CTR with strong CPC, so CTR should always be read alongside CPC and RPM rather than in isolation. It's also easy to accidentally compare CTR across mismatched date ranges, which produces numbers that look meaningful but aren't actually comparable.
