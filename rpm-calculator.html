What is the RPM Calculator?

The RPM Calculator shows how much revenue your site generates for every 1,000 pageviews. RPM, short for Revenue per Mille, is one of the most useful metrics for comparing site-wide performance over time or against other publishers, because it normalizes earnings against traffic volume rather than showing a raw dollar figure that traffic swings can distort.

Enter your total earnings and total pageviews for a period, and the calculator returns your RPM instantly.

Why RPM Matters

Raw earnings alone don't tell you whether your site is monetizing well — a site with rising traffic but flat earnings might actually be monetizing worse per visitor than before. RPM strips out the traffic variable, letting publishers directly compare monetization efficiency across months, content categories, or even different sites in a portfolio.

The Formula

RPM = (Total Earnings ÷ Total Pageviews) × 1,000

Dividing earnings by pageviews gives revenue per single view; multiplying by 1,000 rescales that into the industry-standard "per thousand" format used throughout digital advertising.

Worked Example

A site earning $320 from 40,000 pageviews has an RPM of ($320 ÷ 40,000) × 1,000 = $8.00 — meaning every 1,000 pageviews generates roughly $8 in ad revenue.

How to Use This Calculator

  • Total Earnings: Use your AdSense earnings for the exact date range you're analyzing.
  • Total Pageviews: Use total site pageviews for that same range, from Analytics or AdSense reporting.

RPM is most useful when tracked consistently — recalculate it weekly or monthly using the same methodology so trends are directly comparable.

Tips for Improving RPM

Test ad density carefully — adding more units can raise RPM up to a point, then start hurting user experience and CTR.

Compare RPM by content category to identify which topics monetize best and deserve more editorial investment.

Watch RPM seasonally, since advertiser budgets — and therefore RPM — typically rise in Q4 and dip in slower months.

Common Mistakes

A frequent mistake is confusing site-wide RPM with Page RPM, which uses ad impressions rather than pageviews as the denominator — the two numbers will differ whenever a page shows more or fewer than one ad impression per view. Publishers also sometimes compare RPM across sites with very different traffic sources without accounting for how audience geography and intent affect advertiser demand.
