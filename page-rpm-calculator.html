What is the Page RPM Calculator?

The Page RPM Calculator measures revenue per 1,000 page impressions rather than per 1,000 pageviews. A "page impression" is counted once per page load regardless of how many individual ad units appear on it, which makes Page RPM a slightly different — and often more precise — view of how well a page layout monetizes.

Enter total earnings and total page impressions to see your Page RPM instantly.

Why Page RPM Matters

Because Page RPM is anchored to page loads rather than raw ad impressions, it's especially useful for evaluating layout and page-design changes — like adding, removing, or repositioning ad units — since the pageview-equivalent denominator stays consistent even as ad unit count changes. This isolates the revenue impact of layout decisions more cleanly than metrics tied to individual ad impressions.

The Formula

Page RPM = (Total Earnings ÷ Page Impressions) × 1,000

The structure mirrors standard RPM, but the denominator is specifically page impressions — how AdSense counts a single page load — rather than total site pageviews from an analytics tool, which can sometimes be measured slightly differently.

Worked Example

A page generating $210 in earnings from 30,000 page impressions has a Page RPM of ($210 ÷ 30,000) × 1,000 = $7.00.

How to Use This Calculator

  • Total Earnings: Use earnings attributable to the specific page or page group you're evaluating.
  • Page Impressions: Use the page impressions count from AdSense reporting for that same page or group.

Page RPM is most insightful when compared across similar pages — for example, two article templates with different ad placements — rather than across your entire site at once.

Tips for Improving Page RPM

A/B test ad unit placement on a specific template and compare Page RPM before and after to isolate the layout's true impact.

Use Page RPM, not raw earnings, when deciding whether a redesign improved or hurt monetization on a like-for-like basis.

Segment Page RPM by device type, since mobile and desktop layouts often render a different number of visible ad units.

Common Mistakes

The most common mistake is using site-wide pageviews instead of page-impression counts specific to the page being analyzed, which produces a misleading number. It's also easy to mistake Page RPM for standard RPM in reporting — the two metrics use different denominators and will rarely match exactly, especially on pages with more or fewer than one ad impression per view.
